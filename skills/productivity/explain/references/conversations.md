# Conversation interpretation

Use for understanding a discussion's claims, reasoning, evidence, and changes in direction. Match the depth to the request. A short outcome-only recap needs a summary rather than a full chronological treatment.

## Read the evidence

Read supplied exchanges in chronological order. For message screenshots, capture visible speakers, times, text, chart labels, filters, and numbers; leave obscured or unread material unknown. Use the source's meaning for labels and acronyms, or qualify an ambiguous interpretation.

Keep three distinctions clear when they matter:

- Observed: directly visible in the exchange, query, or chart.
- Inferred: a plausible interpretation of the visible evidence.
- Unconfirmed: a hypothesis, proposal, or detail needing verification.

Preserve agreements, rebuttals, caveats, and the reason the group moved to its next question. A participant's “might,” “I think,” or “could” remains uncertain in the explanation. Explain a speaker's apparent intent as an interpretation, not as access to their private thoughts.

## Explain the exchanges

For a substantive discussion, start with a brief overview of its current question, strongest visible turning point, and unresolved issue or proposed action. Then group connected messages in source order, retaining speaker and time when they clarify the argument.

For each group, explain whether it adds a claim, rebuttal, evidence, caveat, or next action. When translation is requested, give a faithful natural translation followed by its meaning; keep useful technical labels and uncertainty intact. Use the user's language and requested format.

For a query or chart, first explain what it computes or displays, then explain what the participant infers from it. Preserve the visible population, aggregation unit, and time range. Distinguish raw records, transformed or backfilled data, simulations, and live results when the source makes that distinction. Check visible axes, outliers, filters, and population changes before describing a trend. An aggregate time series alone does not establish a record-by-record match.

## State the interpretation boundary

When the discussion could be mistaken for a proven cause, distinguish:

```text
Observed pattern → proposed explanation → validation still needed
```

Name missing data sources, versions, query definitions, or field meanings only when they affect the conclusion. A persuasive clue still needs causal evidence before becoming a confirmed cause. Conclude with the current supported conclusion or the remaining validation when useful. Explanation alone does not start a new investigation, query, implementation, or persistent note.
