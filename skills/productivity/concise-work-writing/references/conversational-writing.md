# Conversational workplace writing

Use for text the user will send as themselves. Choose the voice for this reader and purpose; the assistant's own explanations keep their existing conversational guidance.

## Common approach

- Prefer direct everyday verbs and natural sentences. Contractions such as `I'm`, `I've`, `we're`, and `don't` are normal when they fit the writer and context.
- Preserve the writer's directness, politeness, and uncertainty. Keep a real request clear without automatically turning it into an assignment, a stronger objection, or a weaker suggestion.
- Keep greetings, thanks, and small conversational openings when they do useful social work. Use shared context only when established. A short message does not need a formal introduction or closing.
- Use plain paragraphs for connected thoughts. Use bullets for several distinct items, not merely because the destination is Slack or Jira. Short acknowledgments can stay short.
- Include technical names, error text, dates, or measurements when they help the recipient answer or act. Match explanations to what the reader is known to understand; conversational tone still permits necessary precision.
- `--` and other dashes are optional punctuation. Preserve them when they work; do not impose a ban, a quota, or a signature punctuation habit.
- Let supplied samples guide the result. Correct accidental typos or awkward grammar while retaining intent and rhythm. Do not manufacture slang, jokes, emojis, lowercase habits, or repeated catchphrases to make a message seem personal.

## Adapt to the situation

| Situation | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| Jira progress comment | Outcome or current status, a useful finding/link, and the supported next step or blocker. A dated update label is useful for a chronology; a one-line reply needs no template. |
| Jira answer or discussion | Answer the actual point, then explain the reason or ask the focused follow-up. A comment does not start ticket decomposition or repeat the ticket's sections. |
| One-to-one request | The reason, requested help, and necessary timing or flexibility. Mention earlier agreement only when established. Preserve urgency when real; add none when absent. |
| Broad-channel inquiry | Problem or context, relevant scope/attempts, and one concrete question. Help an unfamiliar reader decide whether they can answer. Courtesy does not require a long preamble or blanket tagging. |
| Broad-channel sharing | Resource or outcome, practical relevance, and the supplied reference. A short list helps when several resources or points matter. Keep an optional invitation distinct from required action. |
| Familiar team conversation | Use the immediate discussion and concise replies. Small acknowledgments or mild informality can fit; a private channel label alone does not establish closeness. |

A long technical reply can use headings or code when the task needs them. A requested manager report can retain grouped progress bullets even when sent as a DM. Audience and purpose take precedence over the service's name.

## Personal voice guidance

If the environment configures a private profile, read only the relevant context and source examples. Explicit preferences outrank inferred habits. Treat account-authored writing as evidence of accepted usage, not proof that every sentence was written without assistance. Use brief spontaneous replies to understand conversational rhythm and longer posts to understand structure; avoid turning a single example into a universal rule.

Profile absence is not a blocker. Keep personal and company samples out of distributable skill sources and personal-device exports unless the applicable policy and user request permit them. Collect or update examples only when requested; ordinary drafting does not authorize searching message history or silently changing a profile.

## Meaning check

Compare the draft to the supplied material: `may` versus `will`, `looking into` versus `fixing`, a question versus a commitment, local tests versus production results, and a proposed deadline versus an agreed one. A natural-sounding sentence that changes these facts needs correction.

Synthetic examples illustrate form, not personal facts or fixed templates:

- Small DM: “Could you check the date range? I used the last full week.”
- Progress comment: “The error message now identifies the missing field. Validation is still pending.”
- Public question: “Does this export include archived records? I'm comparing two exports and need the same scope.”
- Team reply: “That works for me -- I can check it tomorrow.” The timing and commitment must already be supplied.
