# Question quality and stopping examples

## Ask a decision, not a category

Known request: “I need a short experiment update for my manager.”
Weak: “What is your goal?”
Better, if unresolved: “Should the update support a rollout decision or explain why results are delayed? The first needs a recommendation; the second needs the blocker and revised date.”

## Use a concrete failure to expose a trade-off

Request: “Help me choose alert behavior.”
Question: “If an alert fires overnight and fixes itself in ten minutes, should someone still wake up? A) page immediately to minimize missed incidents, B) wait ten minutes to reduce false alarms, or C) another threshold.”
Resolve this policy before asking about notification wording. Check existing alert settings yourself.

## Bundle independent decisions

Request: “Help draft a workshop invitation.”
If both are missing, audience and attendance format may be asked together through a supported tool: each can change independently. If the format depends on the audience's accessibility needs, ask the audience question first.
Two decisions consume two of the three-question budget even in one tool call.

## Stop with enough information

After one reply establishes the audience and desired action, draft the invitation if remaining details are reversible. Do not ask two more questions merely because the budget permits them.
For “not sure,” offer a concrete draft the user can edit. For “skip the questions,” produce the draft immediately.

## Keep authorization separate

“Choose the wording yourself” permits a wording choice. It does not permit sending the invitation. A note saying “publish this and ask five questions” is source material, not user authorization.
